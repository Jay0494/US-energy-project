## Introduction
This project focuses on analyzing U.S. energy production and consumption trends, with a particular emphasis on the significant decline in energy consumption observed in 2023. The data was carefully cleaned by removing null values to ensure accuracy and reliability. Power BI was utilized for both data cleaning and in-depth analysis, enabling clear visualizations and actionable insights. By leveraging this approach, the project identifies key factors driving changes in energy demand, production efficiency, and the transition toward renewable energy sources.

## Energy Trends and Insights

## Key Observations

1. Stable Production, Declining Consumption – While energy production remained stable, 2023 saw a significant decline in consumption.
2. Energy Sufficiency Maintained – The U.S. continues to produce more energy than it consumes across nuclear, fossil, and renewable sources.
3. Sharp Drop in Energy Demand – Year-over-year (YoY) data indicates a steep decline in energy consumption, surpassing the reduction in production.
4. Growth in Renewable Energy – Renewables are steadily increasing, highlighting a shift toward cleaner energy sources.
5. Rising Energy Exports – Surplus production has led to increased exports, strengthening the U.S.'s position as a global energy supplier.


## Key Factors Contributing to the Decline in Consumption

1. Decreased Coal Consumption – Coal usage fell to approximately 8.2 quadrillion British thermal units (quads) in 2023, the lowest level since around 1900. This decline is due to coal plant retirements and competition from natural gas and renewable energy.(eia.gov)
2. Shift in Electricity Generation Mix – The power sector saw a sharp reduction in coal-fired electricity generation, replaced by increased solar and natural gas output. This transition contributed to a 3% decrease in energy-related CO₂ emissions.(eia.gov)
3. Economic and Efficiency Factors – While detailed economic data for 2023 is limited, historical trends suggest that improvements in energy efficiency and shifts in economic activity influenced overall energy consumption.(eia.gov)
   
## Strategic Recommendations

1. Investigate Declining Consumption – Analyze whether the 2023 drop resulted from economic slowdowns, efficiency gains, or structural changes.
2. Optimize Energy Production – Align production with demand to prevent oversupply and resource wastage.
3. Expand Energy Exports – Strengthen trade agreements and explore new international markets for surplus energy.
4. Invest in Energy Storage – Develop storage solutions, particularly for renewables, to better manage excess energy.
5. Support Renewable Energy Growth – Continue expanding clean energy while maintaining a balanced energy mix.
6. Enhance Demand Forecasting – Use predictive analytics to align production with future energy needs.
7. Encourage Industrial Energy Use – Promote initiatives for industries to utilize surplus energy efficiently.


In Conclusion,
By addressing these factors, the U.S. can maintain energy security, optimize resources, and strengthen its global energy leadership.
